\section{Additional Results and Applicability}
\subsection{Complete group-count comparison}
A method comparison needs both favorable and unfavorable cases. Table~\ref{tab:all-band} gives every tested group count for the frozen electrical design, rather than only the selected equivalents. The response features need 4, 8 and 4 groups for S1, S2 and S3 to keep the upper-band PCC active-power error below 5\% at both inputs. The parameter features need 4, 12 and 6 groups. These are the smallest passing values among the tested even group counts.

The coarse two-group equivalent gives a 32.37\% upper-band error in the S2 grouping case. Parameter grouping in S2 gives 14.40\% at four groups and 16.78\% at six groups. Increasing the group count therefore does not ensure decreasing error. In S1, parameter features give smaller band errors than response features at eight and ten groups. Both features use the same physical equivalent construction, so these comparisons concern the grouping rather than different equivalent formulas.

The local voltage response is a more demanding target than the common port. Fig.~\ref{fig:internal} compares the preselected representative chain of S1 with the equivalent containing its hall. This chain has the largest full-model voltage excursion. Parameter grouping combines its hall with halls 2 and 5, whereas response grouping keeps it separate. The parameter-feature equivalent gives a 44.85\% dynamic RMS error for this voltage, even though its PCC active-power error is 1.34\%. The response-feature voltage error is 0.15\% because this hall remains a separate group. This accuracy does not establish preservation of the voltages in its other, merged groups. Across the recorded merged-hall comparisons, local voltage errors range from approximately 45\% to 69\%. These errors describe differences relative to the voltage change from equilibrium. They are not a 45\% to 69\% deviation of the absolute operating voltage. A model accepted for PCC response must not be used to infer the voltage stress of an individual merged chain.

\subsection{Numerical checks and cost interpretation}
All six full-model operating points are stable after excluding the common-angle zero mode. The largest remaining real part lies near $-0.117$~s$^{-1}$. At the grouping operating points, the full model has 1873 equations and variables, with initial residual at most $5.8\cdot10^{-12}$. A two-second stationary check gives drift at most $4.5\cdot10^{-13}$. The 12-group port response differs from the full response by at most $9.3\cdot10^{-13}$ in the small-signal comparison.

The six phase-input full-model runs keep the recorded converter-current measure below 0.689~p.u., the modulation ratio below 1.142 and the DC-link voltage between 0.876 and 1.113~p.u. These checks establish the reference model's behavior for the selected inputs. They do not validate converter protection or establish field operating limits.

Serial integration of 61~s takes median times of 17.56, 38.62 and 16.71~s for the full model in S1, S2 and S3. The exact hall equivalents take 12.76, 28.94 and 12.19~s. The smaller models in the main table take approximately 10 to 27~s. The build and integration records, including the three timing rounds, are retained in \path{data/p3_summary.json}. Model size falls more than runtime because phase events and 0.01~s input knots remain in every model. The reported timing therefore supports an implementation-specific reduction by a factor of 1.3 to 1.6.

\subsection{Evidence limits}
The fast inputs combine literature timing with measured node-power levels. They are constructed scenarios. They do not constitute measured PCC transients, measured synchronization across an operating facility, or a high-bandwidth measurement of the NLR training cycles. Inference remains at a constant request rate within each task window.

The main comparisons use one electrical design. Earlier random-design experiments use different, slower input conditions and remain separate historical evidence. They do not establish the same error values for the phase inputs. The quasi-static network represents electromagnetic line behavior only through fixed-frequency impedances. A network with differential electromagnetic states is required to test the resulting approximation across the full 20~Hz band. The supply-chain model excludes switching ripple, device limiting, protection and battery-only operation. The present conclusions concern task-driven PCC quantities under the stated conditions.
