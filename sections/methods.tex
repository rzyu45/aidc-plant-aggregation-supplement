\section{Aggregation and Validation Definitions}
\subsection{Feature construction}
A consistent comparison requires both feature sets to use the same grouping inputs and equivalent construction.
The 19 design features are the nine passive elements, seven bandwidths, PLL filter frequency, feeder length and unit-transformer impedance.
Mean AC loading supplies the operating-point feature.
Each feature is standardized by its common mean and standard deviation over all halls and the three grouping operating points.
The fixed feature vector uses the 19 design features once and the three normalized loading values divided by $\sqrt{3}$.

For response features, $G_{s,mh}(\ii\omega_k)$ is the full model's response from 1~MW at hall $h$ to output $m$, at grouping operating point $s$.
The four outputs are PCC active power, reactive power and voltage, and synchronous-machine frequency.
The 601 logarithmic samples cover 0.1 to 20~Hz.
Define $w_k=1/(2N_b)$ within band $b$, with the bands 0.1 to 3~Hz and greater than 3 to 20~Hz.
The common output scale is
\begin{equation}
\sigma_m^2=\frac{1}{3H}\sum_{s=1}^{3}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\sum_k w_k|G_{s,mh}(\ii\omega_k)|^2.
\end{equation}
For each hall, the real and imaginary parts of $G_{s,mh}\sqrt{w_k/4}/\sigma_m$ are concatenated over frequency and output.
The three scenario blocks are divided by $\sqrt{3}$ and concatenated.
This retains relative gain and phase differences without allowing one output's unit to set the distance.
Test inputs do not enter these scales.

K-means uses Lloyd's algorithm, 20 initializations and random seed 20260919.
Each medium-voltage section forms $K/2$ groups.
Cross-section merges are prohibited.
Tested total group counts are 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 and 12.
Fixed and per-scenario memberships coincide for this electrical design.
The exact memberships and feature scales are in \path{data/groups.json}.

\subsection{Physical equivalent}
Let a group contain units with ratings $S_n$ and weights $w_n=S_n/\sum_n S_n$.
Its rating is $S_E=\sum_nS_n$ and its terminal demand is the sum of the member demands.
A capacitance, PI gain or filter cut-off $a$ uses $a_E=\sum_nw_na_n$.
A series resistance or inductance uses
\begin{equation}
a_E=\left(\sum_n\frac{w_n}{a_n}\right)^{-1}.
\end{equation}
These rules act on device per-unit values referred to the member ratings.
For the network branches on the common system base, the equal-loss approximation is
\begin{equation}
Z_E=\frac{\sum_n P_n^2Z_n}{(\sum_nP_n)^2}.
\end{equation}
The weights $P_n$ are the full model's initial active flows.
Feeder flows include cooling, whereas unit-transformer flows include only the corresponding IT chain.
Feeder charging is summed.
The approximation does not guarantee an exact loss match when voltage magnitudes or power factors differ.
Its PCC power offset is retained and reported.

Four identical chains in one hall reduce exactly to a chain on their summed rating under equal inputs and consistent initial states.
The common feeder is unchanged and the four unit transformers form their exact parallel equivalent.
Cross-hall aggregation is approximate because device parameters and individual task disturbances differ.
The main transformers and both medium-voltage buses are retained.

\subsection{Small-signal and modal quantities}
Linearization of $\dot x=f(x,z,u)$ and $0=g(x,z)$ gives a state matrix after removal of the common angle reference and elimination of the nonsingular algebraic block.
With derivatives at the operating point,
\begin{equation}
A=f_x-f_zg_z^{-1}g_x,\qquad B=f_u-f_zg_z^{-1}g_u,\qquad G(s)=C(sI-A)^{-1}B+D.
\end{equation}
The output derivatives include the corresponding elimination of $z$.
Input enters the device equations through the terminal-demand command.
The task response is $G(s)B_c$.

Repeated modes use an invariant-subspace projector $\Pi_c=V_c(W_c^{\mathsf H}V_c)^{-1}W_c^{\mathsf H}$.
For a repeated eigenvalue $\lambda_c$, the cluster residue is $C\Pi_cB$.
Its task-to-output residue follows after multiplication by $B_c$.
The contribution measure is the residue magnitude divided by $|\operatorname{Re}\lambda_c|$.
For the modal scatter plot, marker area uses the largest contribution over all tasks of that model.
The highlighted job's transfer curve is evaluated separately.
This avoids interpreting the scatter as a job-specific mode filter.

The main comparison uses the worst task's normalized complex response error in a band $\mathcal B$,
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_{\mathcal B}=\max_j\left[\frac{\sum_{k\in\mathcal B}|G_{E,j}(\ii\omega_k)-G_{F,j}(\ii\omega_k)|^2}{\sum_{k\in\mathcal B}|G_{F,j}(\ii\omega_k)|^2}\right]^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
The sums weight the saved logarithmic grid points equally.
The worst task is selected separately for each output and model.
The main 5\% criterion applies to PCC active power in the upper band at both grouping and test operating points.
It is a study criterion and does not certify the other outputs.
Peak-gain and peak-frequency deviations use each model's refined peak.
Phase difference uses the full model's peak grid frequency for both models.
These distinct definitions explain why matching a peak gain can coexist with a large complex band error.

\subsection{Local supply-chain modes and network coupling}
The origin of a resonant response must be distinguished from its transmission to the PCC.
The supply-chain equations reproduced from \cite{zhao2026dynamic} use unlimited modulation, a fixed VSI reference frequency $\omega_s$ and prescribed terminal demand.
Let $\bm v_c^\star$ denote the numerator of \eqref{eq:vsi-modulation}.
Substitution into the VSI current equation \eqref{eq:vsi-current} gives
\begin{equation}
v_d\bm m_v=\bm v_c^\star,\qquad v_d\ne0.
\end{equation}
The command $\bm v_c^\star$ depends only on local VSI states and fixed references.
The DC-link voltage therefore cancels from the VSI state equations.
The eight VSI states, two PSU states and two server DC--DC states form a local 12-state subsystem driven by its own terminal-demand command.
Network voltage and AFE states do not enter these equations.

This dependence determines how the local modes enter the plant model.
For $N$ supply chains, let $x_L$ collect their local states and let $x_N$ collect the remaining AFE, DC-link and external-grid states.
After elimination of the network algebraic variables, ordering the states as $x_L,x_N$ gives
\begin{equation}
A=\begin{bmatrix}A_L&0\\A_{NL}&A_N\end{bmatrix},\qquad
A_L=\operatorname{diag}(A_{L,1},\ldots,A_{L,N}),
\end{equation}
where each $A_{L,n}$ is the 12-state matrix of one supply chain.
For fixed local operating points and parameters, its eigenvalues do not depend on $A_N$.
Local state disturbances can drive the AFE and DC-link states through $A_{NL}$ and appear at the PCC.
Network coupling remains in $A_N$ and affects the upstream responses.
Thus, a local VSI--PSU mode can appear in several grid-connected quantities without being generated by feedback between supply units.
Signed sums of spectral-projector entries are not energy fractions.

The aggregation and task-allocation comparisons assess task-to-PCC accuracy.
They do not include a controlled removal or variation of network feedback.
They therefore do not isolate inter-unit network coupling as the cause of the dominant resonances or aggregation errors.
This structural interpretation applies to the stated unlimited, fixed-frequency model.

\subsection{Time integration and timing}
Every model receives the same stored input realization.
A one-second rest precedes the 60-second task window.
Every phase transition appears in the scheduled-event calendar.
The integrator stops at the event, installs the next continuous segment, solves consistent algebraic initial values and resumes.
A finite-width ramp is not substituted for a phase transition.

The production calculation uses Solverz Rodas with KLU, JIT-compiled modules, relative tolerance $10^{-4}$, absolute tolerance $10^{-6}$, maximum step 0.01~s and output spacing 0.0025~s.
Consistent initialization uses tolerance $10^{-6}$.
Model checks require equal equation and variable counts and initial residual below $10^{-10}$.
Strict comparisons use relative tolerance $10^{-8}$ and absolute tolerance $10^{-10}$.
The exact 12-group control also uses a paired rerun at relative tolerance $10^{-6}$ to resolve errors near the production tolerance.

For output $y$, set $d_F(t)=y_F(t)-y_F(0)$ and $d_E(t)=y_E(t)-y_E(0)$.
Over the task window, the dynamic error is $\operatorname{RMS}(d_E-d_F)/\operatorname{RMS}(d_F)$.
Initial offset $y_E(0)-y_F(0)$, absolute RMS error and maximum dynamic error are separate quantities.
Raw plots retain the initial offsets.
Internal voltage comparisons map a full-model chain to the equivalent containing that chain's hall; they assess a local-state approximation, not an additional PCC measurement.

Integration timing runs serially.
Each model is built once and warmed up once.
Three subsequent rounds rotate the model order and report the median and range.
The recording function retains PCC active power.
Model building and response calculations are excluded from these integration times.
These are implementation-specific costs rather than estimates from state count.
